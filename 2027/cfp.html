Workshop Scope and Goals

The computing landscape is undergoing unprecedented transformation and heterogeneous systems are the new standard. Driven by critical demands for enhanced energy efficiency, memory capacity, and computational throughput, modern architectures are evolving at every level. Traditional homogeneous designs can no longer meet today's demands of computational throughput, memory bandwidth, and energy efficiency.

Heterogeneous computing harnesses diverse processing units and memory technologies within hierarchical systems to accelerate varied applications. Fields ranging from AI and big data to cloud computing, edge computing, real-time systems, and high-performance computing have achieved significant gains through heterogeneous architectures. ML/AI has become the fastest-moving driver of heterogeneous hardware, and energy has become its binding constraint: leadership-class systems are designed to power envelopes of tens of megawatts, per-accelerator power draw has exceeded a kilowatt, and for large-scale ML/AI what can be deployed is increasingly determined by available power. The efficiency gains now available derive from specialization - domain-specific accelerators, reduced precision, near-memory and dataflow designs, and power-aware scheduling - which places energy efficiency squarely within the scope of this workshop. Additionally, emerging hardware such as wafer-scale accelerators, near-/in-memory processing, disaggregated and CXL-based systems, smart NICs and switches, photonic computing, and dataflow-based accelerators that depart from the traditional von Neumann architecture promise both new challenges and opportunities.

These new heterogeneous architectures demand new applications and programming models to fully utilize their capabilities. This workshop examines heterogeneous design implications across the entire computing stack - from hardware and compiler optimizations to application development and programming environments. It seeks to ground heterogeneous system design research through studies of application kernels and/or whole applications, as well as shed light on new tools, libraries, and runtime systems that improve the performance, energy efficiency, and productivity of applications on heterogeneous systems.

The goal of this workshop is to bring together researchers and practitioners who are at the forefront of heterogeneous computing to learn the opportunities and challenges in future heterogeneous system design trends and thus help influence the next trends in this area.

For AsHES'27 we particularly encourage submissions on (1) ML/AI on heterogeneous systems, (2) energy-efficient methods for HPC and ML/AI systems, and (3) the use of emerging hardware features, devices, and accelerators.

Topics of interest include (but are not limited to):

Applications and Workloads

  • Applications leveraging GPUs, FPGAs, TPUs, DPUs, on-chip accelerators, or other novel architectures
  • AI/ML workloads including generative AI training/inference (e.g. LLMs, LDMs), recommendation systems, and AI for science and engineering
  • Applications and workloads on emerging hardware: wafer-scale accelerators, near-/in-memory processing, disaggregated/CXL systems, smart NICs/switches, photonic computing
  • Scientific and engineering applications
  • Big data and cloud computing workloads
  • Edge computing and real-time applications
  • Application behavior characterization and performance analysis
  • Domain-specific applications and benchmarks

Systems Architecture and Design

  • Heterogeneous system architectures (ARM, RISC-V, custom extensions)
  • Memory hierarchies and novel memory systems, including near-/in-memory processing and disaggregated/CXL memory
  • Energy efficiency and thermal considerations
  • Energy-efficient methods for HPC and ML/AI systems: domain-specific accelerators, reduced precision, near-memory and dataflow designs, power capping and power-aware scheduling
  • Security and reliability in heterogeneous systems
  • Design space exploration and trade-off analysis
  • Performance and power modeling and prediction
  • Emerging accelerator architectures (dataflow, wafer-scale, neuromorphic, photonic, etc.)

Programming Models and Development Tools

  • Programming models and interfaces (CUDA, HIP, OpenMP, OpenACC, SYCL, OneAPI, Kokkos, Raja)
  • Debugging and profiling tools
  • Performance and energy optimization and autotuning frameworks
  • Testing and verification methods for heterogeneous systems
  • Workload characterization tools
  • Benchmarking suites and methodologies
  • Heterogeneous system software and runtime systems
  • Compiler optimizations for heterogeneous systems

Runtime Systems and Resource Management

  • Operating system support for heterogeneous computing
  • Virtualization and containerization for heterogeneous systems
  • Task scheduling and load balancing, including power-aware and energy-aware scheduling
  • Memory management and data movement optimization
  • System-level monitoring, telemetry, and energy measurement
  • Integration of heterogeneous components and interfaces
  • Communication software/middleware for heterogeneous systems, including smart NICs and switches
